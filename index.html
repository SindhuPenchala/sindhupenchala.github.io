What I work on

Four threads, one question: how can machines see enough of the world to act well in it?

Behavior and engagement

Datasets and transformer models that recognize gaze, collaboration, and engagement in children, and early signs of aggression in video.

  • ChildGaze
  • Learning in Focus
  • Pre-incident prediction

Seeing from very little

Reconstructing and classifying surfaces when only 10% of the image is visible, and fusing touch with vision.

  • One Patch
  • SSUF
  • Surformer v2

Safe AI and robots

Zero-trust, multi-gate safety for language models, now extended to action-level checks for vision-language-action robots.

  • Cognitive Firewall
  • Cognitive Autonomy
  • VLA action gates

Security analytics

Machine learning that ranks vulnerabilities by real-world exploit risk, grounded in four years of patching at enterprise scale.

  • Beyond CVSS
  • WBTS model

I'm a computer vision researcher at the University of Alabama's BRAINS Lab. My doctoral work starts from ChildGaze, a dataset of how children look and collaborate, and builds toward social robots that notice behavioral patterns over time and respond in the right way.

Before research, I spent four years at Tata Consultancy Services as a patching and vulnerability analyst, where I also served as backup lead and change coordinator. That work taught me to care about systems that hold up in the real world, and it still shapes how I evaluate models today.
